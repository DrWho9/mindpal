## 3. What "Option A" means here

**Option A (CONTINUITY preferred framing):** MindPal is a *loneliness-adjacent digital wellbeing companion*. It is **not** treatment of a diagnosed condition, it has no locked clinical indication, and it makes no TGA claim.

Why this fits a First Nations focus without overclaiming:

- AIHW separates SEWB and wellbeing from diagnosed mental illness (S1). Option A sits on the wellbeing side, which is where MindPal honestly is.
- The AIHW data on low perceived social support (S1) connects to the loneliness-adjacent framing **as need only**. MindPal has no evidence that it changes social support (UNKNOWN).
- The First Nations–led ecosystem already exists (S2). MindPal's honest role is to be a daily companion that **points people toward** those resources, not a new "Indigenous mental health app".

### Proposed sequence (intent only)

1. **Listen first.** Seek guidance from First Nations–led organisations and people with lived experience before designing anything. BDI's Lived Experience Centre and Network pages (S2) show why this comes first. This is awareness only. **No contact has been made.**
2. **Agree governance and data terms** with those involved, including who can stop the work.
3. **Co-design review of shipped features** (Companion tone, Journal prompts, Help listings, imagery). Pay participants for their time (budget UNKNOWN).
4. **Pathway layer first.** With community review, add First Nations–led support listings (candidates from S2: WellMob, headspace yarn safe, Head to Health's First Nations page, NIPS/Thirrili, Gayaa Dhuwi). This complements existing resources and does not replace them.
5. **Content gate.** No First Nations content, scripts or imagery until they are made or approved by First Nations people. DNB-18 stays behind this gate.
6. **Only then** consider a small, community-agreed trial. Any evaluation would be designed with the community. **No outcomes are claimed in the grant.**
