If you have noticed mood changes that seem related to your menstrual cycle, you can bring those observations to a qualified health professional. You do not have to work out a diagnosis yourself.

This guide does not decide whether you have PMS, PMDD, or another condition. MindPal does not predict your cycle or recommend medicines or hormonal treatment.

If keeping notes feels useful, you might record a date, what you noticed, and whether it affected something in daily life. You can keep the note brief. You do not have to record bleeding, intimate details or any information you would rather keep out of an app.

Brief notes may help start a conversation, but they are not a diagnostic assessment. A clinician may suggest daily symptom recording across at least two menstrual cycles to understand a pattern. You do not need to wait or finish a diary before asking for help, especially if symptoms are severe or disrupting daily life.

You can also prepare a question without keeping a diary: I have noticed changes in my mood and would like help understanding them. Or, what information would be useful for our next appointment?

Your experience does not have to fit a regular cycle to deserve attention. You can mention uncertainty, irregular periods, or other changes to the clinician. MindPal will not interpret those details for you.

If recording makes you more distressed or preoccupied, stop. You can ask for support without collecting more information first.

This is an optional pathway. You decide what to save and share with a clinician. If you think you may act on thoughts of suicide or self-harm, or you are in immediate danger, call triple zero in Australia. For crisis support, call Lifeline on 13 11 14. You can also find support options in “Help me now”. MindPal does not monitor you or contact help for you.
