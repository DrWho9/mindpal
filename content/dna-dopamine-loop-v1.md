## Full talk-through (reading / Companion)

### Opening
This is an optional MindPal talk-through about **drugs and alcohol** — sometimes people shorten that to “DNA,” meaning substances, not your genes.  
It is **general education**, not medical advice, diagnosis, or a treatment plan. If you are dependent, withdrawing, or in danger, please use real human help and local emergency services.

### The puppy-on-the-couch picture
Imagine a puppy messes on the couch, and someone gives it a treat right after. The puppy doesn’t think “I was naughty.” It learns: *that sequence got a reward.* So the behaviour can show up more often.

Bodies and brains learn in a similar way. They notice what followed what — especially when a strong reward chemical message (often talked about as **dopamine**) shows up.

### Feeling low → substance → brief “up”
When you feel down, flat, anxious, or ashamed, a drink or a stimulant can bring a fast shift — numbness, energy, or relief. That relief is a powerful signal.  

Your system can start to pair:  
**“I felt awful → I used → I felt different.”**

That pairing is training. Not because you are weak — because reward learning is how brains work.

### Why the spiral can tighten
After the short lift, mood often drops again (sometimes lower than before). If the next answer is another drink or line, the loop runs again:

1. Feel rough  
2. Use for a quick change  
3. Brief reward signal  
4. Come down / feel rough again  
5. Stronger urge to repeat  

Over time, that loop can make the “need something” pathway feel louder, and ordinary good things (rest, food, friends, a win) feel quieter by comparison. People sometimes describe this as the brain getting more practised at the low-mood-plus-use route.

### What this is *not* saying
- It does not mean one drink “ruins” you.  
- It does not replace assessment by a clinician or AOD service.  
- Withdrawal can be medically serious — get proper help; don’t white-knuckle dangerous stops alone.

### A gentler retrain (literacy, not a protocol)
You don’t have to fix everything today. A small contrary pairing helps the learning system notice another option, for example:
- Feel rough → glass of water / step outside / text a safe person / one line in your MindPal wins → *then* decide about using  
- Or: urge shows up → delay ten minutes + name the feeling in Journal  

The point is to stop *only* rewarding the use-after-pain path — and to give your brain other “what happened next” stories.
